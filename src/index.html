Is Freight Moving?

Freight is the economy you can weigh: tonnes on trucks and rails, containers on flatcars, miles on highways. It is the physical twin of the refining margin tracked at diesel, and it stops before the wider economy notices. This page keeps the score: truck tonnage, rail carloads and intermodal since 2000 with every freight recession dated and scored, the deep flanking series back to 1967, and Canada's rail tonnage. Nothing here is a forecast.

Three episodes in the measurable era, and each tells a different story. The 2006 alarm led the Great Recession by 22 months, freight's famous early call, and the merged 2006-09 episode swallowed a 2008 dead-cat bounce on its way to the deepest bottom in the series. The 2020 stop was coincident: the recession caused it, not the reverse. …: …

What is absent matters as much. The 2015-16 industrial bust and the 2019 trucking slump are both visible in the rail and Cass lines on this page, but neither held truck tonnage below its year-earlier level for three straight months, so under the printed rule neither qualifies; absence under a printed rule is information about where those downturns actually lived. And the 2001 recession goes uncredited (…) because the tonnage series starts in 2000: its first measurable year IS that recession, with no crest visible before it, the same left-censoring the lending page declares for 1990.

The composite BTS actually publishes

The Freight Transportation Services Index: trucking, rail, inland waterways, pipeline and air freight in one seasonally adjusted index, monthly since 2000, revised routinely as source data settle (the revision log below records it).

The recent tape: Cass shipments and expenditures

Ten years is all there is: Cass licenses FRED to redistribute only a sliding window (2016 onward as probed), although the index reaches 1990. Shown because the pair is the industry's benchmark, and the gap between the two lines is freight rates: expenditures rising faster than shipments is pricing power, the reverse is a rate bust.

The deep flankers

Heavy truck sales, SAAR, since 1967

Vehicle miles traveled, YoY, since 1971

Heavy-truck sales are the capex canary: fleets stop ordering before freight turns, and the series has crashed into or through every recession of its six decades. Vehicle miles are everything on wheels, not just freight, drawn year-over-year because the source is unadjusted; the 2020 crater is the deepest reading in either series' history.

Canada: the railway economy

Total railway traffic carried, tonnes, year over year, monthly since 1999. Canada's freight economy is disproportionately rail (grain, potash, oil, containers from the ports), and the source is unadjusted, so year-over-year is the honest rendering. Shading is the C.D. Howe chronology.

Sources and construction

United States

  • Bureau of Transportation Statistics via FRED, all monthly since 2000, seasonally adjusted: freight TSI TSIFRGHT, truck tonnage TRUCKD11 (the scored series), rail carloads RAILFRTCARLOADSD11, intermodal RAILFRTINTERMODALD11.
  • The deep flankers: heavy truck sales HTRUCKSSAAR (since 1967) and vehicle miles TRFVOLUSM227NFWA (since 1970).
  • Cass Freight Index shipments FRGSHPUSM649NCIS and expenditures FRGEXPUSM649NCIS: redistribution limited to a sliding ~10-year window (probed 2026-09-07), stated wherever drawn.
  • AAR's weekly rail carloads have no keyless machine-readable feed (press releases only); monthly BTS rail carries that story here, and the weekly gap is named rather than scraped around.
  • Recession bands: NBER dates via USREC, from the shared dataset.

Canada

  • Railway carloadings, total traffic carried in tonnes, Statistics Canada table 23-10-0216-01, vector v74869, resolved from cube metadata and fetched with its live English title verified. Monthly since 1999, unadjusted.
  • Recession bands: C.D. Howe Institute Business Cycle Council chronology, from the shared dataset.

Plumbing

  • FRED is fetched keyless-first (public CSV), keyed API as fallback; StatCan WDS has no keys. A failed fetch carries the stored series forward and says so.
  • Refreshes daily via cron; BTS posts mid-month, StatCan on its own calendar. The stamp above says when data last moved.
  • Series follow the shared econ-core contract used across lending, credit, housing, yield, jobs, debt and diesel, with a vintages field reserved for as-published views.
  • Raw data: /api/data, or the files under /data/.
  • Assembled with Claude (Anthropic). Measured volumes and computed history with the rule printed; no forecasts.
